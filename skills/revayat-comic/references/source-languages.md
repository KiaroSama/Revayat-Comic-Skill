# Source languages and Persian voice

Read the common pass and the section for the actual source language before
translating a page. These are original adaptations of the inspected workflows
listed in the repository's `docs/research/` notes; they are guidance, not a
claim of bilingual validation or an installed external model.

## Common pass: every language to Persian

Translate directly from the source into Persian. English may be a reading aid,
never a compulsory intermediate translation. Use the declared language and
locale plus the page as evidence: Latin letters do not establish English;
Han characters alone do not distinguish Chinese, Japanese and Hanja.
Mixed-language regions need individual readings. For an unfamiliar language,
consult its primary grammar/dictionary and record unresolved ambiguity rather
than silently applying the English profile.

Read the whole panel and linked balloons before drafting, keeping each original
region ID. Use the nearest relevant context, the approved glossary and known
speaker/addressee relationships. Existing title policy and cast decisions win
over generic language advice. Do not infer a relationship just from apparent
age, gender, rank or bubble shape.

Draft the full meaning in natural Persian. Everyday Iranian dialogue may be
conversational; a formal speaker, narrator or quoted document keeps its register.
Keep a compact voice record: speaker, addressee, تو/شما, formality, characteristic
wording, evidence page and confidence. Record a relationship change when the
source changes it. Do not turn a foreign regional accent into an unrelated
Iranian accent. Preserve an insult's target, force and function without adding
abuse or softening it automatically.

Review source/target accuracy first, then Persian fluency, voice and terminology.
For a doubtful span, identify a concrete possible error and revise only with
source/scene evidence. More critique calls or back-translation are not proof.
Fit after semantic review; do not let balloon size delete conditions, negation,
participants, quantities or deliberate pauses. Log the decision and any correction
beside the output, following `translation-log.md`.

## Japanese (`ja`)

Verify vertical columns and balloon order; inspect furigana/ruby, small kana,
dakuten and hand-lettered effects at native detail. Ruby can supply a second
meaning or a special name reading, not just redundant pronunciation. Record
both when relevant. Japanese OCR is optional evidence and can hallucinate on
text-free art; it cannot establish reading order or speaker identity.

Resolve omitted participants from the panel and context. Preserve contrastive
pronouns even where Persian could omit them. Negated necessity differs from
prohibition; verb endings and honorific choices signal relationships. `-san`
alone establishes neither gender nor a Persian Mr/Mrs title. Use the book's
established honorific and name policy consistently.

## Chinese (`zh`, including Simplified and Traditional)

Establish language, word segmentation and reading direction before interpreting
Han-only text. Vertical text can read down columns ordered right-to-left;
Traditional/Simplified is independent of direction. Preserve the actual source
characters; automatic conversion can obscure a proper name or title.

Distinguish 不必 (lack of necessity) from 不准 (prohibition), and review negation
scope, aspect, result and participant contrast. Retain known source-script names,
approved readings and Persian forms in the glossary. Genre titles, sect ranks
and kinship terms depend on the story's relationships and conventions.

## Korean (`ko`)

Inspect Hangul syllable components, isolated jamo, meaningful spacing and Hanja.
Do not apply Japanese whitespace stripping to Korean. Speech endings carry
politeness and a change in relationship; render that through Persian address,
verbs and vocabulary without mechanically adding شما to every line.

Kinship terms can be social address: 언니 alone does not prove biological
sisterhood. Distinguish permission not to act from an instruction not to act.
Keep established family/given-name order and Persian spellings; English
romanization is a reading aid, not a reason to rename the cast every chapter.

## French (`fr`)

`vous` may be respectful singular or plural. Preserve a meaningful tu/vous
switch through Persian address, agreement and register. Review negation scope:
`ne...que` restricts, `ne...plus` ends a prior state, expletive `ne` is not
negative, and colloquial omission of `ne` does not remove the negative meaning.
Unresolved `plus` can have opposite readings; flag it when the scene cannot decide.

Keep accents, apostrophes and interruptions in transcription. Preserve explicit
daughter/son or other identity contrasts even though Persian pronouns are
gender-neutral. Translate idioms and expletives by scene function, including
visual literal puns, rather than choosing the strongest dictionary sense.

## Spanish (`es`)

Use locale and scene for tú/usted/vos/ustedes. `usted` addresses the listener;
`ustedes` can be ordinary informal plural in Latin America; familiar American
`vos` and historical reverential `vos` are different. Preserve the relationship,
not an automatic formal/informal lookup.

Negative concord stays negative. A negated necessity construction can remove
an obligation or prohibit an action depending on context; `no tener que` is not
an unconditional mapping. Resolve omitted subjects and `su` from evidence,
keeping uncertainty where needed. Preserve accents and ¿/¡ in transcription,
using Persian punctuation in the target. Region-dependent idioms/profanity
need the country and scene; a literal visual joke can override an idiom reading.

## English (`en`) and other sources

Preserve modal force, sarcasm, phrasal-verb meaning, ambiguous referents and
split-balloon continuity. English `you` does not determine Persian تو/شما;
the relationship does. Contractions alone do not license extra slang. For any
other source language, follow the common pass and add only verified language
notes needed for this book. Never substitute Arabic for a provider missing
Persian support. A provider's language list is not a quality certificate.
